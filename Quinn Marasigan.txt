        <h2>Introduction</h2>
        <p>A pediatrician is a medical doctor who specializes in the health and well-being of infants, children, and teenagers. From newborn checkups to treating everyday illnesses and tracking growth milestones, a pediatrician is often the first doctor a child ever knows &mdash; and sometimes the person a worried parent trusts most. Out of every path in medicine, this is the one I keep coming back to.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="why" class="band band-sage">
      <div class="wrap">
        <h2>Why I Chose This Career</h2>
        <p>Ever since I was young, I have loved being around children, from my younger cousins to the kids in my neighborhood. I still remember how gentle and patient my own pediatrician was when I was scared of getting a vaccine shot, and I want to give that same comfort to another child one day. Science has always been one of my favorite subjects, especially biology, so a career that combines my love for kids with my curiosity about the human body feels like the perfect fit. I also want a job where I can see the direct result of my work, and I cannot think of anything more rewarding than sending a healthier, happier child home to their family.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="skills" class="band band-cream">
      <div class="wrap">
        <h2>Skills Needed</h2>
        <ul class="skills-list">
          <li>Strong communication, to explain things clearly to both children and worried parents</li>
          <li>Patience and a calm temperament, especially with scared or crying patients</li>
          <li>Empathy and compassion, to comfort children and reassure their families</li>
          <li>Sharp attention to detail, for spotting symptoms that could signal something serious</li>
          <li>Problem-solving, to figure out what is wrong even when a young patient can't explain it</li>
          <li>Physical and mental stamina, for long shifts and emotionally demanding days</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="education" class="band band-sage">
      <div class="wrap">
        <h2>Education &amp; Qualifications</h2>
        <p>Becoming a pediatrician is a long road, but every step builds the knowledge needed to care for children safely. It starts with a four-year bachelor's degree, usually with a strong foundation in biology and chemistry. Next comes four years of medical school to earn a Doctor of Medicine (MD) degree, followed by a three-year pediatric residency where new doctors train directly with patients in hospitals and clinics. Finally, pediatricians must pass a licensing exam and can choose to become board-certified, showing that they meet the highest standards of pediatric care. Altogether, it takes more than a decade of study and training, but I see that time as an investment in every child I will one day help.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="responsibilities" class="band band-cream">
      <div class="wrap">
        <h2>Career Responsibilities</h2>
        <ul class="resp-list">
          <li>Performing routine checkups and tracking a child's growth and development</li>
          <li>Diagnosing and treating common childhood illnesses, from ear infections to asthma</li>
          <li>Giving vaccines and keeping track of each child's immunization schedule</li>
          <li>Educating parents about nutrition, safety, and healthy habits at home</li>
          <li>Referring patients to specialists when a condition needs more advanced care</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>
